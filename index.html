        How the numbers work

        Where every number on this site comes from, how good the estimates are, and what the planner can’t do.

        What this does differently

        Most calculators start from a lender’s ratio of your pay before taxes. This one starts from what is left each month after your real bills. It also explains each home’s estimate, shows a range instead of one number, and runs entirely on your device.

        Where the homes come from

        1. Homes. Forsyth County tax assessor parcel records, tax year 2025: all homes, each with its county value, size, age, lot and location. These are county records, not listings, so most homes are not for sale.
        2. School zones. Forsyth County GIS “School Districts” layer (last edited March 27, 2026). Zones can change, so check with the school district.
        3. Not included. Homes built or split after the 2025 tax roll aren’t in the data. Homes built in 2025 may have been valued while still being built.

        How the estimate is built

        A LightGBM model (600 decision trees) learned the county’s value from each home’s size, age, floors, lot, use type, neighborhood, tax class, zoning, construction grade, structure type and location. It was checked with 5-fold cross-validation: each home is scored by a model that never saw it. The same trees run in your browser, which is how the “What pushed the estimate” bars and the what-if sliders work.

        How accurate it is

        The model predicts the county’s assessed value, so these numbers measure how close it gets to that value. They are not comparable to listing sites’ accuracy numbers, which measure sale prices.

          From county value to a likely sale price

          How the monthly cost works

          1. Price used. Each home’s likely sale price (above), unless you type an asking price on the home.
          2. Loan payment. The standard fixed-rate formula: payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly rate and n the number of monthly payments.
          3. Property tax. Georgia taxes 40% of a home’s value, times the local millage rate (dollars per $1,000 of assessed value), divided by 12.
          4. Home insurance. An average cost per $1,000 of value, unless you type in a real quote.
          5. Mortgage insurance. Only when the down payment is under 20% of the price.
          6. HOA dues. Only if you type them in on a home.
          7. Down payment. Your savings minus closing costs, the way the CFPB suggests working it out, but never under the minimum down payment.

          What it can’t do

          • It is a planning tool, not financial advice, a loan offer, or an appraisal.
          • It doesn’t know a home’s condition, updates, or whether it is for sale.
          • It can’t tell whether a home is inside the City of Cumming, which adds a city tax rate.
          • It can’t check whether you qualify for a loan or a program. Ask a lender or a HUD-approved housing counselor.
          • Shown values are rounded: home values to the nearest $1,000 in lists and ranges, monthly costs to the nearest $1. The math keeps full precision.

          Privacy

          Nothing you type is sent anywhere or saved. Refreshing or closing the page clears it. There are no accounts, cookies, or analytics.
