Abode

A home-buying worksheet for Forsyth County, Georgia families: start from your monthly budget and see what a home would really cost each month.

Most tools ask how big a loan you could qualify for. Abode asks what you can afford comfortably: how much of your take-home pay is left after every bill, including the home.

Start with your budget Or look up a home
  1. Budget. Take-home pay, debts, other costs and savings. You get the most you could comfortably spend on housing each month, and the price that points to.
  2. Homes. Every home in Forsyth’s 2025 tax records, on a map and in a list, with the ones that fit your budget and how many fit in each school zone.
  3. Detail. One home’s estimate and range, its full monthly cost, the cash needed up front, and what-if checks like a higher rate or a drop in income.

What this does differently

Most calculators start from a lender’s ratio of your pay before taxes. This one starts from what is left each month after your real bills. It also explains each home’s estimate, shows a range instead of one number, and runs entirely on your device.

Privacy

Nothing you type is sent anywhere or saved. Refreshing or closing the page clears it. There are no accounts, cookies, or analytics. The one thing the site saves is the list of homes you add to compare (their parcel IDs only), in this browser, until you clear it. The page loads its files (including fonts and icons) only from this site, and its security policy blocks requests to anywhere else. Links you choose to click, like the county record, open other sites.

What it can’t do

  • It is a planning tool, not financial advice, a loan offer, or an appraisal.
  • It doesn’t know a home’s condition, updates, or whether it is for sale.
  • It can’t tell whether a home is inside the City of Cumming, which adds a city tax rate.
  • It can’t check whether you qualify for a loan or a program. Ask a lender or a HUD-approved housing counselor.
  • Shown values are rounded: home values to the nearest $1,000 in lists and ranges, monthly costs to the nearest $1. The math keeps full precision.

Buying for the first time?

Down payment
The part of the price you pay up front from savings. The loan covers the rest.
Closing costs
Fees paid when the purchase is finished, on top of the down payment, such as lender and title fees. The (i) next to Section B shows the share this planner uses and its source.
PMI (private mortgage insurance)
Insurance that protects the lender, not you. It is usually required when the down payment is under 20% of the price, and it adds to the monthly payment.
Debt-to-income (DTI)
Your monthly debt payments, including the new home payment, divided by your monthly pay before taxes. Lenders use it to decide how much to lend. This planner looks at what is left of take-home pay instead.

      How the monthly cost works

      1. Price used. Each home’s likely sale price (above), unless you type an asking price on the home.
      2. Loan payment. The standard fixed-rate formula: payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan, r the monthly rate and n the number of monthly payments.
      3. Property tax. Georgia taxes 40% of a home’s value, times the local millage rate (dollars per $1,000 of assessed value), divided by 12.
      4. Home insurance. An average cost per $1,000 of value, unless you type in a real quote.
      5. Mortgage insurance. Only when the down payment is under 20% of the price.
      6. HOA dues. Only if you type them in on a home.
      7. Down payment. Your savings minus closing costs, the way the CFPB suggests working it out, but never under the minimum down payment.

      How the estimate is built

      A LightGBM model (600 decision trees) learned the county’s value from each home’s size, age, floors, lot, use type, neighborhood, tax class, zoning, construction grade, structure type and location. It was checked with 5-fold cross-validation: each home is scored by a model that never saw it. The same trees run in your browser, which is how the “What pushed the estimate” bars and the what-if sliders work.

      How accurate it is

      The model predicts the county’s assessed value, so these numbers measure how close it gets to that value. They are not comparable to listing sites’ accuracy numbers, which measure sale prices.

          About Abode

          A home-buying worksheet for Forsyth County, Georgia families: start from your monthly budget and see what a home would really cost each month.

          Why I built this

          Most affordability tools answer “what is the biggest loan we qualify for?” I wanted one that answers “what can we afford comfortably?” It starts from what a family earns and spends each month, shows which Forsyth homes fit that budget without leaving them short, and what each home would really cost per month.

          It is a planning tool, not financial advice, an appraisal, or a loan offer.

          Who it’s for

          • Families in or near Forsyth County thinking about buying a first or next home.
          • People who have already looked at listing sites and want to check whether a price is reasonable and whether they can carry it.

          How accurate the estimates are

          The model predicts the county’s assessed value, so these numbers measure how close it gets to that value, not to sale prices. They come from the notebook (housing_model_v2.ipynb), where every home was scored by a model that hadn’t seen it (5-fold cross-validation).

          What the 80% range means

          Each estimate comes with a range, from 8.5% below to 11.0% above it. The range was set from the model’s misses on homes it hadn’t seen, so that about 8 in 10 county values land inside it. When that was checked on a held-out half of the homes, 80% did. It works better for some price tiers than others:

          What each set of inputs added

          Not tested yet for Forsyth: how the model does on whole neighborhoods it has never seen (the notebook ran that only for Alpharetta), and how close estimates are to Forsyth sale prices (there are no Forsyth sale prices in the open data).

          Limits

          • It is a planning tool, not financial advice, a loan offer, or an appraisal.
          • Estimates follow the county’s 2025 assessed values. The likely sale price divides them by 0.78, a ratio measured on 2025 Alpharetta sales, because Forsyth has no open sale records.
          • Homes built or split after the 2025 tax roll aren’t in the data, and homes built in 2025 may have been valued while still being built.
          • The monthly cost leaves out the homestead exemption, the City of Cumming tax rate, utilities and upkeep.
          • It doesn’t know a home’s condition or whether it is for sale, and it can’t check whether you qualify for a loan or a program.
